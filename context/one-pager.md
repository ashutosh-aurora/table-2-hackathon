# Offsite one-pager — picking a problem to work on

*Pre-shared primer, read before the day. The agent's `plan-the-session` and `land-on-problem` skills
work from the same lens and criteria. Owned by Donna + Johannes.*

## Why we're here today

We are here to:
- align on the direction we want to go — Aurora's priorities, articulated more clearly in terms of the
  use cases we want to enable for our clients and the capabilities we want to embody and champion;
- explore a new way of working together — with colleagues we don't often work with, and with Claude in
  the mix;
- and because we believe this new way of working could significantly change and improve how knowledge
  and value move through us.

*(The morning strategy session sets the fuller direction — see `context/business/`.)*

## What makes a good problem to pick

We want problems that **live the ethos of the day** — that truly leverage who's at the table, and that
point our attention clearly at the goals: tackling Aurora priorities, leveraging the new way of working,
and attacking historically thorny problems that have bottlenecked our collective impact.

A good candidate is:
- **Genuinely felt** — someone real wants it solved.
- **Historically too daunting** — something important we never attempted because it seemed too big.
- **Sliceable in a day** — a slice is doable today; even a slice that just *illustrates the principle*
  counts, if it helps more people understand and shape it.

## Where your team starts

Your table has been put together with a light theme — it's in `context/product-area/theme.md`.

We don't want to be prescriptive: the prompts below are starting points; the agent can generate more
from the two sections above.
- **Close to use cases** → product-integration opportunities in client use cases (external UX seams).
- **A capability team, further from use cases** → internal interface / process improvement, within your
  team or with the adjacent end-user team (not an external client).

## How to choose

The goal of the day is to have fun, so choose **what the table is most excited to work on** — not some
hard objective value score. It still has to be genuinely felt; excitement is how you choose among the
ones that are.

## Where to look

Start from your theme in `context/product-area/theme.md`, then widen using the business and
product-area context. Explore several candidate problems before you settle — the right one is usually a
seam between use cases, products, or teams.
